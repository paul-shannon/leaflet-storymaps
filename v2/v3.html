Raven's Journey

This sculpture was created by Marvin Oliver especially to honor and inspire students at the University of Washington. At a celebration of its installation, he said,

“The canoe is a means of sharing… food, regalia, love for our countrymen, our environment. It is critical to make the canoe move. That means you need to have paddles. Those paddles are your education. And it takes a collective effort for your education.

The canoe is your journey. You’ll be pullers, and as you graduate you will be a skipper. You will have your family, your friends, and the career. Those are the ones that will help you make it work.

There’s days you can paddle far. There’s days you cannot paddle. It’s an amazing exciting venture.”

The following description of the elements in the sculpture is also provided by the artist, Marvin Oliver:

  • THE RAVEN: Denotes the symbol of humor, cultural hero, and transformer. The Raven, like us, changes to adapt to the environment. I see this as similar to students’ path at the University,, growing, learning adapting and moving forward in their journey.
  • THE SALMON: Represents our most vital resource and provider of life. It is a symbol of abundance and prosperity. We honor the salmon and give back during the salmon ceremony.
  • THE CANOE: A mode of transportation, the canoe, also bonded extended families and was used traditionally as a burial site connecting us to our ancestors. This Salish design (form) is unique to our region.
  • THE WOLF: Depicts our guardian. He/she is the provider of food, home and spirituality, a symbol of strength in family, kinship and unity.
  • THE MOON: The protector and the guardian of the night. The moon protects your journey.”

Location: The work is on the far south wall of the University of Washington Student Union Building, the “HUB,” 4001 E Stevens Way NE, Seattle, WA 98195.

This art was made possible by the student funding of the HUB renovation in 2010-2012.
